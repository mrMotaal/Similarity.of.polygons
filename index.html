Geometric Similarity in Daily Life & Technology

Why do we study similarity? When two objects share the exact same shape but differ in size, mathematical similarity preserves identical angle measures and constant proportional side ratios. Explore three essential everyday applications:

1 Heritage Sculptures & 3D Miniature Replicas Proportional Scaling

A seated historical stone statue stands at a grand height of 195 cm with a base width of 58 cm. A specialized museum workshop produces mathematically similar miniature souvenir models with a base width of exactly 5 cm. What will be the exact height of the miniature replica?

Proportional Solution:
$$\frac{\text{Height of Model}}{\text{Height of Statue}} = \frac{\text{Width of Model}}{\text{Width of Statue}} \implies \frac{h}{195} = \frac{5}{58} \implies h = \frac{5 \times 195}{58} \approx 16.81\text{ cm}$$
2 Smartphone Photography & Optical Screen Rescaling Aspect Ratio

A high-resolution rectangular street billboard banner measures 12 meters wide by 8 meters tall. When captured on a smartphone screen without cropping or stretching, its image is scaled down proportionally to a width of 6 cm. What is the height $h$ of the image on the screen?

Constant Aspect Ratio Solution:
$$\frac{\text{Height on Screen}}{\text{Height of Billboard}} = \frac{\text{Width on Screen}}{\text{Width of Billboard}} \implies \frac{h}{8} = \frac{6}{12} \implies h = \frac{6 \times 8}{12} = 4\text{ cm}$$
3 Architectural Blueprints & Sports Field Cartography Scale Drawing ($k$)

A school sports court has actual field dimensions of 40 meters length and 25 meters width. An engineer drafts a geometrically similar blueprint on drafting paper with a length of 16 cm. What is the required width $w$ on the drawing paper?

Blueprint Scale Factor Solution:
$$\frac{\text{Blueprint Width}}{\text{Actual Field Width}} = \frac{\text{Blueprint Length}}{\text{Actual Field Length}} \implies \frac{w}{25} = \frac{16}{40} = \frac{2}{5} \implies w = 25 \times \frac{2}{5} = 10\text{ cm}$$

Core Mathematical Foundation

Two necessary conditions that must both be satisfied simultaneously

1 Corresponding Angles are Congruent

The measure of every interior angle in the first polygon is equal to its corresponding angle in the second:

$$m(\angle A) = m(\angle E),\quad m(\angle B) = m(\angle F),\quad m(\angle C) = m(\angle G),\quad m(\angle D) = m(\angle H)$$
2 Corresponding Side Lengths are Proportional

The ratio of each side length to its corresponding side is a constant scale factor $k$:

$$\frac{AB}{EF} = \frac{BC}{FG} = \frac{CD}{GH} = \frac{DA}{HE} = k$$
$k$ is termed the Similarity Scale Factor (or Constant of Proportionality)
Enlargement Polygon 1 is an enlargement of Polygon 2
$k > 1$
Reduction Polygon 1 is a shrunk copy of Polygon 2
$0 < k < 1$
Congruence Polygons are identical in shape and size
$k = 1$
Fundamental Invariant Perimeter Theorem

The ratio between the perimeters of two similar polygons equals the ratio between any two corresponding sides:

$$\frac{\text{Perimeter of Polygon 1}}{\text{Perimeter of Polygon 2}} = k = \frac{\text{Side}_1}{\text{Side}_2}$$

Idea 1: Verifying Both Similarity Conditions

Angle congruency and side length proportionality

1.1 Worked Example 1: Verifying Scaled Quadrilaterals

In the given figure, prove that polygon $ABCD \sim \text{polygon } EFGH$, and determine the similarity scale ratio of polygon $ABCD$ to polygon $EFGH$.

Educator Note: Always verify that corresponding vertices match in sequence: $A \leftrightarrow E,\ B \leftrightarrow F,\ C \leftrightarrow G,\ D \leftrightarrow H$.
Solution Workspace
Formal Proof Walkthrough:

1. Angle Congruence: $m(\angle A) = m(\angle E),\ m(\angle B) = m(\angle F),\ m(\angle C) = m(\angle G) = 90^\circ,\ m(\angle D) = m(\angle H)$.

2. Side Proportionality: $\frac{AB}{EF} = \frac{12}{8} = \frac{3}{2},\ \frac{BC}{FG} = \frac{9}{6} = \frac{3}{2},\ \frac{CD}{GH} = \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2},\ \frac{DA}{HE} = \frac{15}{10} = \frac{3}{2}$.

$$\therefore \text{Polygon } ABCD \sim \text{Polygon } EFGH,\quad k = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5$$

1.2 Worked Example 2: Proving Similarity of Scaled Polygons

In the given figure, polygon $ABCD$ and polygon $KLMN$ have right angles at $C$ and $M$ respectively, with corresponding angles congruent. Prove that polygon $KLMN \sim \text{polygon } ABCD$, and find their similarity scale ratio.

Solution Workspace
Step-by-Step Proof:

1. $m(\angle C) = m(\angle M) = 90^\circ$ and all corresponding angles match.

2. $\frac{KL}{AB} = \frac{16}{24} = \frac{2}{3},\ \frac{LM}{BC} = \frac{16}{24} = \frac{2}{3},\ \frac{MN}{CD} = \frac{8}{12} = \frac{2}{3},\ \frac{NK}{DA} = \frac{12}{18} = \frac{2}{3}$.

$$\therefore \text{Polygon } KLMN \sim \text{Polygon } ABCD,\quad k = \frac{2}{3}$$

1.3 Try It Yourself Verifying Polygons with Proportional Sides

In the given figure, prove that polygon $ABCD \sim \text{polygon } HLKJ$, and state the similarity scale ratio of polygon $ABCD$ to polygon $HLKJ$.

Solution Workspace
Model Solution:

1. $m(\angle D) = m(\angle J) = 92^\circ$ and all corresponding angles match.

2. $\frac{AB}{HL} = \frac{5}{10} = \frac{1}{2},\ \frac{BC}{LK} = \frac{5}{10} = \frac{1}{2},\ \frac{CD}{KJ} = \frac{5}{10} = \frac{1}{2},\ \frac{DA}{JH} = \frac{2.5}{5} = \frac{1}{2}$.

$$\therefore \text{Polygon } ABCD \sim \text{Polygon } HLKJ,\quad k = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5$$

02

Idea 2: Finding Unknown Side Lengths & Angle Measures

Cross-multiplication with radicals and quadrilateral interior angles

2.1 Worked Example 1: Unknown Side Lengths and Interior Angles

Given that polygon $ABCD \sim \text{polygon } EFGH$: Find $m(\angle C)$, $m(\angle D)$, and the exact lengths of $DC$ and $FG$.

Solution Workspace
Model Solution:

$m(\angle C) = 45^\circ,\ m(\angle D) = 360^\circ - (90^\circ + 120^\circ + 45^\circ) = 105^\circ$.

Scale factor $k = \frac{15\sqrt{3}}{5\sqrt{3}} = 3$. Hence $FG = \frac{30}{3} = 10\text{ cm}$ and $DC = 3 \times 10\sqrt{2} = 30\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}$.

2.2 Worked Example 2: Angle Variables and Proportional Sides

Quadrilateral $ABCD \sim \text{quadrilateral } PQRS$. Given $m(\angle B) = 116^\circ,\ m(\angle C) = 61^\circ,\ m(\angle R) = (y - 73)^\circ,\ AB = 10\text{ cm},\ BC = 12\text{ cm},\ PQ = 5\text{ cm},\ QR = x\text{ cm}$, find $y$ and $x$.

Solution Workspace
Model Solution:

$y - 73 = 61 \implies y = 134$.

$\frac{PQ}{AB} = \frac{QR}{BC} \implies \frac{5}{10} = \frac{x}{12} \implies x = 6\text{ cm}$.

2.3 Try It Yourself Scale Factor & Unknown Side Computations

Polygon $ABCD \sim \text{polygon } EFGH$, with scale factor $k = \frac{3}{2}$. Find $m(\angle C)$, $m(\angle D)$, side $AD$, and side $FG$.

Solution Workspace
Model Solution:

$m(\angle C) = m(\angle G) = 135^\circ$, and $m(\angle D) = 360^\circ - (78^\circ + 85^\circ + 135^\circ) = 62^\circ$.

$AD = 4\sqrt{3} \times \frac{3}{2} = 6\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}$ and $FG = \frac{16\sqrt{2}}{3/2} = \frac{32\sqrt{2}}{3}\text{ cm}$.

Idea 3: Special Quadrilateral Conditions

Necessary and sufficient criteria for specific polygons

3.1 Worked Example 1: Rectangle Dimension Proportionality

Rectangle $ABCD$ has length $BC = 6\text{ cm}$ and width $CD = 4\text{ cm}$. Rectangle $XYZM$ has length $YZ = 3\text{ cm}$ and width $ZM = 2\text{ cm}$. Prove that rectangle $ABCD \sim \text{rectangle } XYZM$, and state their scale factor.

Solution Workspace
Proof Walkthrough:

1. All 4 interior angles are $90^\circ$.

2. $\frac{BC}{YZ} = \frac{6}{3} = 2$ and $\frac{CD}{ZM} = \frac{4}{2} = 2 \implies k = 2$.

3.2 Worked Example 2: Rhombus Similarity Evaluation

Rhombus $ABCD$ has side length $3\text{ cm}$ and $m(\angle D) = 110^\circ$. Rhombus $EFGH$ has side length $5\text{ cm}$ and $m(\angle G) = 70^\circ$. Prove that Rhombus $ABCD \sim \text{Rhombus } EFGH$.

Solution Workspace
Proof Walkthrough:

$m(\angle C) = 180^\circ - 110^\circ = 70^\circ$, matching $m(\angle G) = 70^\circ$. Scale factor $k = \frac{3}{5}$.

3.3 Try It Yourself Parallelogram Similarity Criteria

Parallelogram $ABCD$ ($AB = 8,\ BC = 6,\ m(\angle B) = 120^\circ$) and Parallelogram $PQRS$ ($PQ = 6,\ QR = 4.5,\ m(\angle P) = 60^\circ$). Prove they are similar and find $k$.

Solution Workspace
Proof Walkthrough:

$m(\angle A) = 180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ$. Scale factor $k = \frac{8}{6} = \frac{4}{3}$.

04

Idea 4: Perimeter Ratios & Algebraic Expressions

Solving for variables $x, y, z$ using perimeter ratio invariant $k$

4.1 Worked Example 1: Algebraic Variables with Scale Ratio 3 : 2

Polygon $ABCD \sim \text{polygon } EFGH$ with perimeter ratio $\frac{3}{2}$. Find $x$, $y$, and $z$.

Solution Workspace
Model Solution:

$k = \frac{3}{2}$. $\frac{6}{z} = \frac{3}{2} \implies z = 4$.

$\frac{y+3}{5} = \frac{3}{2} \implies y = 4.5$. $\frac{2x+1}{x+2} = \frac{3}{2} \implies x = 4$.

4.2 Worked Example 2: Determining Polygon Side Lengths via Given Perimeter

Two similar polygons. First pentagon sides: $3, 5, 6, 8, 10\text{ cm}$. Second polygon perimeter: $80\text{ cm}$. Determine scale factor and side lengths.

Solution Workspace
Model Solution:

$\text{Perimeter}_1 = 32\text{ cm}$. $k = \frac{80}{32} = 2.5$.

Sides: $7.5\text{ cm},\ 12.5\text{ cm},\ 15\text{ cm},\ 20\text{ cm},\ 25\text{ cm}$.

4.3 Try It Yourself Multi-Variable Algebraic System with Ratio 3 : 4

Polygon $ABCD \sim \text{polygon } EFGH$ with perimeter ratio $\frac{3}{4}$. Find $x$, $y$, and $z$.

Solution Workspace
Model Solution:

$k = \frac{3}{4}$. $\frac{2z-3}{8} = \frac{3}{4} \implies z = 4.5$.

$\frac{x+1}{2x} = \frac{3}{4} \implies x = 2$. $\frac{4.5}{2y} = \frac{3}{4} \implies y = 3$.

Engineering & Display Aspect Ratio Application

Geometric similarity in modern multimedia hardware

Real-World Application: Cinema Screen Aspect Ratio Aspect Ratio

A cinema theater screen is a rectangle with width 45 feet and height 25 feet. Which TV screen format—Format A ($36\text{ inches} \times 20\text{ inches}$) or Format B ($36\text{ inches} \times 27\text{ inches}$)—is mathematically similar to the cinema screen to prevent crop distortion or black bars?

Solution Workspace
Aspect Ratio Verification:
$$\text{Cinema Ratio} = \frac{45}{25} = 1.8$$ $$\text{Format A Ratio} = \frac{36}{20} = 1.8,\quad \text{Format B Ratio} = \frac{36}{27} \approx 1.33$$

Verdict: Format A is geometrically similar because its dimension ratio identically equals $1.8$.
