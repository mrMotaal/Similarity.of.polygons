Geometric Similarity in Daily Life & Technology

Why do we study similarity? When two objects share the exact same shape but differ in size, mathematical similarity preserves identical angle measures and constant proportional side ratios. Explore three essential everyday applications:

1 Heritage Sculptures & 3D Miniature Replicas Proportional Scaling

A seated historical stone statue stands at a grand height of 195 cm with a base width of 58 cm. A specialized museum workshop produces mathematically similar miniature souvenir models with a base width of exactly 5 cm. What will be the exact height of the miniature replica?

Proportional Solution:
$$\frac{\text{Height of Model}}{\text{Height of Statue}} = \frac{\text{Width of Model}}{\text{Width of Statue}} \implies \frac{h}{195} = \frac{5}{58} \implies h = \frac{5 \times 195}{58} \approx 16.81\text{ cm}$$
2 Smartphone Photography & Optical Screen Rescaling Aspect Ratio

A high-resolution rectangular street billboard banner measures 12 meters wide by 8 meters tall. When captured on a smartphone screen without cropping or stretching, its image is scaled down proportionally to a width of 6 cm. What is the height $h$ of the image on the screen?

Constant Aspect Ratio Solution:
$$\frac{\text{Height on Screen}}{\text{Height of Billboard}} = \frac{\text{Width on Screen}}{\text{Width of Billboard}} \implies \frac{h}{8} = \frac{6}{12} \implies h = \frac{6 \times 8}{12} = 4\text{ cm}$$
3 Architectural Blueprints & Sports Field Cartography Scale Drawing ($k$)

A school sports court has actual field dimensions of 40 meters length and 25 meters width. An engineer drafts a geometrically similar blueprint on drafting paper with a length of 16 cm. What is the required width $w$ on the drawing paper?

Blueprint Scale Factor Solution:
$$\frac{\text{Blueprint Width}}{\text{Actual Field Width}} = \frac{\text{Blueprint Length}}{\text{Actual Field Length}} \implies \frac{w}{25} = \frac{16}{40} = \frac{2}{5} \implies w = 25 \times \frac{2}{5} = 10\text{ cm}$$

Core Mathematical Foundation

Two necessary conditions that must both be satisfied simultaneously

1 Corresponding Angles are Congruent

The measure of every interior angle in the first polygon is equal to its corresponding angle in the second:

$$m(\angle A) = m(\angle E),\quad m(\angle B) = m(\angle F),\quad m(\angle C) = m(\angle G),\quad m(\angle D) = m(\angle H)$$
2 Corresponding Side Lengths are Proportional

The ratio of each side length to its corresponding side is a constant scale factor $k$:

$$\frac{AB}{EF} = \frac{BC}{FG} = \frac{CD}{GH} = \frac{DA}{HE} = k$$
$k$ is termed the Similarity Scale Factor (or Constant of Proportionality)
Enlargement Polygon 1 is an enlargement of Polygon 2
$k > 1$
Reduction Polygon 1 is a shrunk copy of Polygon 2
$0 < k < 1$
Congruence Polygons are identical in shape and size
$k = 1$
Fundamental Invariant Perimeter Theorem

The ratio between the perimeters of two similar polygons equals the ratio between any two corresponding sides:

$$\frac{\text{Perimeter of Polygon 1}}{\text{Perimeter of Polygon 2}} = k = \frac{\text{Side}_1}{\text{Side}_2}$$

Idea 1: Verifying Both Similarity Conditions

Angle congruency and side length proportionality

1.1 Worked Example 1: Verifying Scaled Quadrilaterals

In the given figure, prove that polygon $ABCD \sim \text{polygon } EFGH$, and determine the similarity scale ratio of polygon $ABCD$ to polygon $EFGH$.

Educator Note: Always verify that corresponding vertices match in sequence: $A \leftrightarrow E,\ B \leftrightarrow F,\ C \leftrightarrow G,\ D \leftrightarrow H$.
Student Scratchpad & Solution Workspace 16-Line Ruled Pad
Formal Proof Walkthrough:

1. Angle Congruence: By inspection of corresponding vertices:

$$\begin{aligned} m(\angle A) &= m(\angle E), & m(\angle B) &= m(\angle F) \\ m(\angle C) &= m(\angle G) = 90^\circ, & m(\angle D) &= m(\angle H) \end{aligned}$$

2. Side Proportionality: Computing side ratios in order:

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{AB}{EF} &= \frac{12}{8} = \frac{3}{2}, & \frac{BC}{FG} &= \frac{9}{6} = \frac{3}{2} \\[4pt] \frac{CD}{GH} &= \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2}, & \frac{DA}{HE} &= \frac{15}{10} = \frac{3}{2} \end{aligned}$$

$$\therefore \text{Polygon } ABCD \sim \text{Polygon } EFGH,\quad k = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5$$

1.2 Worked Example 2: Proving Similarity of Scaled Polygons

In the given figure, polygon $ABCD$ and polygon $KLMN$ have right angles at $C$ and $M$ respectively, with corresponding angles congruent. Prove that polygon $KLMN \sim \text{polygon } ABCD$, and find their similarity scale ratio.

Educator Note: All side lengths are indicated directly on the figure. Observe the sequence of vertices in the required similarity statement.
Student Scratchpad & Solution Workspace 16-Line Ruled Pad
Step-by-Step Proof:

1. Angle Congruence: Corresponding angles are congruent: $m(\angle C) = m(\angle M) = 90^\circ$ and all corresponding angles match.

2. Side Proportionality: Computing side ratios from polygon $KLMN$ to polygon $ABCD$:

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{KL}{AB} &= \frac{16}{24} = \frac{2}{3}, & \frac{LM}{BC} &= \frac{16}{24} = \frac{2}{3} \\[4pt] \frac{MN}{CD} &= \frac{8}{12} = \frac{2}{3}, & \frac{NK}{DA} &= \frac{12}{18} = \frac{2}{3} \end{aligned}$$

$$\therefore \text{Polygon } KLMN \sim \text{Polygon } ABCD,\quad k = \frac{2}{3}$$

1.3 Try It Yourself Verifying Polygons with Proportional Sides

In the given figure, prove that polygon $ABCD \sim \text{polygon } HLKJ$, and state the similarity scale ratio of polygon $ABCD$ to polygon $HLKJ$.

Educator Note: Observe vertex alignment: $A \leftrightarrow H,\ B \leftrightarrow L,\ C \leftrightarrow K,\ D \leftrightarrow J$.
Student Scratchpad & Solution Workspace 16-Line Ruled Pad
Step-by-Step Proof:

1. Angle Congruence: Corresponding angles are congruent: $m(\angle D) = m(\angle J) = 92^\circ$, and all other corresponding angles match by geometric alignment.

2. Side Proportionality: Computing side ratios in corresponding vertex order:

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{AB}{HL} &= \frac{5}{10} = \frac{1}{2}, & \frac{BC}{LK} &= \frac{5}{10} = \frac{1}{2} \\[4pt] \frac{CD}{KJ} &= \frac{5}{10} = \frac{1}{2}, & \frac{DA}{JH} &= \frac{2.5}{5} = \frac{1}{2} \end{aligned}$$

$$\therefore \text{Polygon } ABCD \sim \text{Polygon } HLKJ,\quad k = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5$$

Idea 2: Finding Unknown Side Lengths & Angle Measures

Cross-multiplication with radicals ($\sqrt{3}, \sqrt{2}$) and quadrilateral interior angles

2.1 Worked Example 1: Unknown Side Lengths and Interior Angles

Given that polygon $ABCD \sim \text{polygon } EFGH$:

  • Find $m(\angle C)$ and $m(\angle D)$.
  • Find the exact lengths of side $DC$ and side $FG$.
Student Scratchpad & Solution Workspace 16-Line Ruled Pad
Step-by-Step Model Solution:

1. Angle Calculations:

$$m(\angle C) = m(\angle G) = 45^\circ,\quad m(\angle A) = m(\angle E) = 90^\circ,\quad m(\angle B) = m(\angle F) = 120^\circ$$ $$m(\angle D) = 360^\circ - (90^\circ + 120^\circ + 45^\circ) = 360^\circ - 255^\circ = 105^\circ$$

2. Side Lengths via Scale Factor:

$$k = \frac{AB}{EF} = \frac{15\sqrt{3}}{5\sqrt{3}} = 3$$ $$\frac{BC}{FG} = 3 \implies FG = \frac{30}{3} = 10\text{ cm}$$ $$\frac{DC}{HG} = 3 \implies DC = 3 \times 10\sqrt{2} = 30\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}$$
2.2 Worked Example 2: Angle Variables and Proportional Sides

In the given figure, quadrilateral $ABCD \sim \text{quadrilateral } PQRS$. Given that $m(\angle B) = 116^\circ$, $m(\angle C) = 61^\circ$, $m(\angle R) = (y - 73)^\circ$, $AB = 10\text{ cm}$, $BC = 12\text{ cm}$, $PQ = 5\text{ cm}$, and $QR = x\text{ cm}$, find the numerical value of $y$ and the side length $x$.

Educator Note: Equate corresponding angles to solve for $y$, then apply side proportionality to determine $x$.
Student Scratchpad & Solution Workspace 16-Line Ruled Pad
Step-by-Step Model Solution:

1. Solving for $y$ via Angle Congruence:

$$m(\angle R) = m(\angle C) \implies y - 73 = 61 \implies y = 61 + 73 = 134$$

2. Solving for $x$ via Side Proportionality:

$$\frac{PQ}{AB} = \frac{QR}{BC} \implies \frac{5}{10} = \frac{x}{12} \implies x = \frac{5 \times 12}{10} = 6\text{ cm}$$
2.3 Try It Yourself Scale Factor & Unknown Side Computations

In the given figure, polygon $ABCD \sim \text{polygon } EFGH$, and the scale factor of polygon $ABCD$ to polygon $EFGH$ is $\frac{3}{2}$.

  • Find $m(\angle C)$ and $m(\angle D)$.
  • Find the exact lengths of side $AD$ and side $FG$.
Student Scratchpad & Solution Workspace 16-Line Ruled Pad
Step-by-Step Model Solution:

1. Angle Calculations:

$$m(\angle C) = m(\angle G) = 135^\circ$$ $$m(\angle D) = 360^\circ - (78^\circ + 85^\circ + 135^\circ) = 360^\circ - 298^\circ = 62^\circ$$

2. Side Lengths via Given Scale Factor $k = \frac{3}{2}$:

$$\frac{AD}{HE} = \frac{3}{2} \implies \frac{AD}{4\sqrt{3}} = \frac{3}{2} \implies AD = \frac{3 \times 4\sqrt{3}}{2} = 6\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}$$ $$\frac{BC}{FG} = \frac{3}{2} \implies \frac{16\sqrt{2}}{FG} = \frac{3}{2} \implies FG = \frac{2 \times 16\sqrt{2}}{3} = \frac{32\sqrt{2}}{3}\text{ cm}$$

Idea 3: Special Quadrilateral Conditions

Necessary and sufficient criteria for specific polygons

All Squares
Always Similar

Every square has four $90^\circ$ right angles and equal sides, satisfying both similarity criteria automatically.

Two Rectangles
Dimension Ratio

All angles are $90^\circ$. They are similar if and only if their dimension ratios are proportional:

$$\frac{L_1}{L_2} = \frac{W_1}{W_2}$$
Two Rhombuses
One Angle Pair

All 4 sides are equal in each rhombus. They are similar if and only if one pair of corresponding angles is congruent:

$$m(\angle A) = m(\angle E)$$
Regular Polygons
Same $n$ Sides

Any two regular polygons with the exact same number of sides $n$ are always geometrically similar (e.g. regular hexagons $n=6$).

$$\text{Interior Angle } = \frac{(n - 2) \times 180^\circ}{n} = 120^\circ$$
3.1 Worked Example 1: Rectangle Dimension Proportionality

Rectangle $ABCD$ has dimensions length $BC = 6\text{ cm}$ and width $CD = 4\text{ cm}$. Rectangle $XYZM$ has dimensions length $YZ = 3\text{ cm}$ and width $ZM = 2\text{ cm}$. Prove that rectangle $ABCD \sim \text{rectangle } XYZM$, and state their similarity scale factor.

Student Scratchpad & Solution Workspace 16-Line Ruled Pad
Proof Walkthrough:

1. In both rectangles, all 4 interior angles are identically $90^\circ$, satisfying the angle congruency condition automatically.

2. Check the ratio of corresponding dimensions:

$$\frac{BC}{YZ} = \frac{6}{3} = 2,\quad \frac{CD}{ZM} = \frac{4}{2} = 2 \implies \frac{BC}{YZ} = \frac{CD}{ZM} = 2$$

$\therefore \text{Rectangle } ABCD \sim \text{Rectangle } XYZM,\quad k = 2$.

3.2 Worked Example 2: Rhombus Similarity Evaluation

Rhombus $ABCD$ has side length $3\text{ cm}$ and $m(\angle D) = 110^\circ$. Rhombus $EFGH$ has side length $5\text{ cm}$ and $m(\angle G) = 70^\circ$. Prove that Rhombus $ABCD \sim \text{Rhombus } EFGH$, and state their similarity ratio.

Student Scratchpad & Solution Workspace 16-Line Ruled Pad
Proof Walkthrough:

1. In rhombus $ABCD$, consecutive interior angles are supplementary: $m(\angle C) = 180^\circ - 110^\circ = 70^\circ$.

2. In rhombus $EFGH$, $m(\angle G) = 70^\circ$. Thus $m(\angle C) = m(\angle G) = 70^\circ$.

3. Since all 4 side lengths are equal within each rhombus, side ratios are identically $\frac{3}{5}$.

$\therefore \text{Rhombus } ABCD \sim \text{Rhombus } EFGH,\quad k = \frac{3}{5}$.

3.3 Try It Yourself Parallelogram Similarity Criteria

Parallelogram $ABCD$ has side lengths $AB = 8\text{ cm}, BC = 6\text{ cm}$ and $m(\angle B) = 120^\circ$. Parallelogram $PQRS$ has side lengths $PQ = 6\text{ cm}, QR = 4.5\text{ cm}$ and $m(\angle P) = 60^\circ$. Prove that Parallelogram $ABCD \sim \text{Parallelogram } PQRS$, and state the similarity scale ratio.

Student Scratchpad & Solution Workspace 16-Line Ruled Pad
Proof Walkthrough:

1. In parallelogram $ABCD$, consecutive angles are supplementary: $m(\angle A) = 180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ$.

2. In parallelogram $PQRS$, $m(\angle P) = 60^\circ$. Thus $m(\angle A) = m(\angle P) = 60^\circ$, making all corresponding interior angles congruent.

3. Check proportionality of adjacent side lengths:

$$\frac{AB}{PQ} = \frac{8}{6} = \frac{4}{3},\quad \frac{BC}{QR} = \frac{6}{4.5} = \frac{12}{9} = \frac{4}{3}$$

$\therefore \text{Parallelogram } ABCD \sim \text{Parallelogram } PQRS,\quad k = \frac{4}{3}$.

Idea 4: Perimeter Ratios & Algebraic Expressions

Solving for variables $x, y, z$ using perimeter ratio invariant $k$

4.1 Worked Example 1: Algebraic Variables with Scale Ratio 3 : 2

In the given figure, polygon $ABCD \sim \text{polygon } EFGH$ and the ratio of their perimeters is $\frac{\text{Perimeter of } ABCD}{\text{Perimeter of } EFGH} = \frac{3}{2}$. Find the numerical values of $x$, $y$, and $z$.

Educator Note: The ratio of perimeters equals the constant similarity ratio $k = \frac{3}{2}$ for every pair of corresponding sides.
Student Scratchpad & Solution Workspace 16-Line Ruled Pad
Algebraic Resolution Walkthrough:

Since the perimeter ratio is $\frac{3}{2}$, the similarity ratio $k = \frac{3}{2}$.

1. Solving for $z$:

$$\frac{AB}{EF} = \frac{3}{2} \implies \frac{6}{z} = \frac{3}{2} \implies z = \frac{6 \times 2}{3} = 4$$

2. Solving for $y$:

$$\frac{BC}{FG} = \frac{3}{2} \implies \frac{y + 3}{5} = \frac{3}{2} \implies 2(y + 3) = 15 \implies 2y = 9 \implies y = 4.5$$

3. Solving for $x$:

$$\frac{CD}{GH} = \frac{3}{2} \implies \frac{2x + 1}{x + 2} = \frac{3}{2} \implies 2(2x + 1) = 3(x + 2) \implies 4x + 2 = 3x + 6 \implies x = 4$$
4.2 Worked Example 2: Determining Polygon Side Lengths via Given Perimeter

Two polygons are geometrically similar. The side lengths of the first pentagon are $3\text{ cm}, 5\text{ cm}, 6\text{ cm}, 8\text{ cm}$, and $10\text{ cm}$. The perimeter of the second polygon is $80\text{ cm}$. Determine the similarity scale factor and calculate the lengths of all sides of the second polygon.

Student Scratchpad & Solution Workspace 16-Line Ruled Pad
Step-by-Step Resolution:

1. Compute perimeter of the first polygon:

$$\text{Perimeter}_1 = 3 + 5 + 6 + 8 + 10 = 32\text{ cm}$$

2. Scale factor from first polygon to second polygon:

$$k = \frac{\text{Perimeter}_2}{\text{Perimeter}_1} = \frac{80}{32} = 2.5$$

3. Calculate each corresponding side length in the second polygon:

$$s_1 = 3 \times 2.5 = 7.5\text{ cm},\quad s_2 = 5 \times 2.5 = 12.5\text{ cm},\quad s_3 = 6 \times 2.5 = 15\text{ cm}$$ $$s_4 = 8 \times 2.5 = 20\text{ cm},\quad s_5 = 10 \times 2.5 = 25\text{ cm}$$
4.3 Try It Yourself Multi-Variable Algebraic System with Ratio 3 : 4

In the given figure, polygon $ABCD \sim \text{polygon } EFGH$ and $\frac{\text{Perimeter of } ABCD}{\text{Perimeter of } EFGH} = \frac{3}{4}$. Find the numerical values of the variables $x$, $y$, and $z$.

Educator Note: Form individual algebraic proportions with $k = \frac{3}{4}$ to solve for each variable independently.
Student Scratchpad & Solution Workspace 16-Line Ruled Pad
Step-by-Step Algebraic Resolution:

Since the perimeter ratio is $\frac{3}{4}$, the similarity ratio $k = \frac{3}{4}$.

1. Solving for $z$:

$$\frac{AB}{EF} = \frac{3}{4} \implies \frac{2z - 3}{8} = \frac{3}{4} \implies 4(2z - 3) = 24 \implies 2z - 3 = 6 \implies 2z = 9 \implies z = 4.5$$

2. Solving for $x$:

$$\frac{BC}{FG} = \frac{3}{4} \implies \frac{x + 1}{2x} = \frac{3}{4} \implies 4(x + 1) = 6x \implies 4x + 4 = 6x \implies 2x = 4 \implies x = 2$$

3. Solving for $y$:

$$\frac{CD}{GH} = \frac{3}{4} \implies \frac{4.5}{2y} = \frac{3}{4} \implies 6y = 4.5 \times 4 = 18 \implies y = 3$$

Real-World Application: Cinema Screen Aspect Ratio Aspect Ratio

A cinema theater screen is a rectangle with width 45 feet and height 25 feet. Which TV screen format—Format A ($36\text{ inches} \times 20\text{ inches}$) or Format B ($36\text{ inches} \times 27\text{ inches}$)—is mathematically similar to the cinema screen to prevent crop distortion or black bars?

Student Scratchpad & Solution Workspace 16-Line Ruled Pad
Aspect Ratio Verification:
$$\text{Cinema Ratio} = \frac{45}{25} = 1.8$$ $$\text{Format A Ratio} = \frac{36}{20} = 1.8,\quad \text{Format B Ratio} = \frac{36}{27} \approx 1.33$$

Verdict: Format A is geometrically similar because its dimension ratio identically equals $1.8$.
